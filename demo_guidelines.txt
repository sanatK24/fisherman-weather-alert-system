
        INDIAN MARITIME FISHERIES SAFETY GUIDELINES
              Government of India - Ministry of Fisheries


SECTION 1: GENERAL SAFETY RULES

All fishermen must wear a life jacket before entering the vessel.
It is mandatory to carry a first aid kit on every fishing trip.
You should never go fishing alone, always travel in groups of 2 or more boats.
Every vessel must carry emergency flares and a whistle for signaling.
Ensure that your boat is in proper working condition prior to departure.
Fishing is prohibited during cyclone warnings and red alert days.
Children below 14 years are forbidden from going on fishing trips.

SECTION 2: WEATHER GUIDELINES

Always check the weather forecast before going to sea.
Wind speeds above 45 km/h are dangerous for small boats under 30 feet.
Do not venture into sea during monsoon season between June and September.
Lightning and thunder are signs of an approaching storm. Return to shore immediately.
Fog reduces visibility to less than 500 meter. Use GPS navigation during fog.
If wave height exceeds 3 meter, all small fishing boats should return to harbor.
Temperature drops below 15 degree Celsius can cause hypothermia. Carry warm clothing.
Tidal changes are strongest during full moon. Exercise caution near rocky shores.

SECTION 3: FISHING REGULATIONS

A valid fishing license is required for all commercial fishing activities. Fine for violation is ₹10000.
Minimum net mesh size should be 25 mm to allow juvenile fish to escape.
Maximum catch limit is 100 kg per boat per day for small mechanized vessels.
Fishing is prohibited within 5 km of marine protected areas and wildlife sanctuaries.
Use of dynamite, poison, or electric current for fishing is strictly forbidden. Penalty is ₹50000 and imprisonment.
Turtle nesting beaches are restricted zones from November to March.
Trawling is banned during the 61 day fishing ban period announced by the government.
Fish below minimum size of 20 cm must be released back into the water.

SECTION 4: REQUIRED EQUIPMENT

Every boat must carry the following mandatory safety equipment:
1. Life jackets - minimum 1 per person on board
2. GPS device for navigation and location tracking
3. Marine radio or communication equipment for contacting coast guard
4. Anchor and minimum 50 meter rope
5. Torch light with extra battery for night fishing
6. First aid kit with basic medical supplies
7. Drinking water - minimum 5 liter per person per day
8. Tool kit for basic engine and motor repairs
9. Fire extinguisher - mandatory for all motorized boats
10. Fishing net repair kit with hooks, line, and spare bait

SECTION 5: EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

In case of emergency, immediately contact the Coast Guard on channel 16 or call 1554.
If the boat is capsizing, ensure all crew members wear life jackets and stay together.
Send SOS distress signal using marine radio or emergency flares.
If someone falls overboard, throw a life ring and do not jump in after them.
In case of engine failure, drop anchor immediately to avoid drifting into dangerous areas.
For medical emergency at sea, call the emergency helpline 108 or marine ambulance 1093.
If caught in a storm, head to the nearest harbor. Do not try to ride out the storm at sea.
Always carry a waterproof emergency contact list with these numbers:

EMERGENCY CONTACTS:
- Indian Coast Guard: 1554
- Marine Police Control Room: 100
- Fisheries Emergency: 1800-425-1600
- Emergency Ambulance: 108
- Disaster Management: 1078
- Navy Rescue: 1718

SECTION 6: NAVIGATION RULES
------------------------------
All vessels must carry proper navigation lights during night fishing.
Minimum safe distance between two fishing boats should be 100 meter.
Speed limit within harbor area is 5 km/h to ensure safety of other boats.
Prior to crossing shipping lanes, ensure no large cargo vessels are approaching.
Communication equipment must be switched on at all times during the trip.
